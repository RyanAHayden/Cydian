Safety Nets for Exploitable iOS

Some iOS versions have a public kernel exploit. On those, one bad IPA can take over the phone, and iFizBox makes installing IPAs easy. Below are three levels of protection. Each one includes everything in the level before it. Pick one, or mix pieces.

What is already in iFizBox to build on

Source Warning (core/warnings.js): Canister's piracy list ships inside the app, with a reason and link per host, checked before Add Source fetches anything. A known-safe list and a malware list can work the same way: shipped, offline, and every entry carries a reason and a link.

Reviews (worker/src/reviews.js, D1): no accounts. Each device has its own token and gets one review per app, and 3 reports hide a review. "Report as Malicious" can reuse that table, the token and the rate limits.

A. Quiet: one warning, one blocklist

The app speaks up only twice: once if your iOS version is exploitable, and when an app is on the malware list. Reports from people go to the maintainer and never show in the app.

The known-safe list

It ships in the app, like the piracy list: core/safe-sources.js, one entry per host with a one-line reason and a public link (the project's repo or site). Recommended Sources draws from it, so the two can't drift apart.

Who decides: the maintainer, through a pull request with the link as evidence. The bar: a known developer or team, a public repo or a long track record, and no reports.

Seed list: iFizBox's own source, plus the well-known sideload sources (AltStore's official one, SideStore, Feather, LiveContainer, the big community IPA repos). For jailbreak repos, the default repos (Procursus, Havoc, Chariz) are already treated as essential.

Other lists: I found no maintained public "safe AltStore sources" list to copy, so we would keep our own. If one shows up, a sync script can merge it, as the piracy list does.

Trade-off

  • Answers "can I trust this?" on the page where you decide to install.
  • A list to curate, and reports to review.
  • The report threshold has to resist brigading: 10 distinct devices on one version, a per-network cap, and a developer can ask for a review.

Recommendation: B

B puts the answer on the page where people decide to install, and it never blocks anyone except for confirmed malware. A on its own warns people but gives them nothing to act on. C adds friction most people will turn off. Build B in three steps: (1) the exploitable-iOS list and the one-time alert, (2) the known-safe list and the trust labels, (3) Report as Malicious and the malware list. Each step ships on its own.

On every option, the jailbreak build skips the iOS-version alert, because people stay on exploitable versions to jailbreak. It keeps the labels, reports and malware list.
